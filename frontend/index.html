What you are looking at

NewsMind reads the financial news wire and maps each story to the companies it could move. Nothing here is a buy or sell signal.

Tone, from −1 to +1
How the wording of a headline reads, scored by a model called FinBERT. −1 reads as bad news, 0 as neutral, +1 as good news. It describes the headline, not what to do with the stock.
Arrows ▲ ▼
Claude's judgement of which way a story is likely to push a company's share price. A solid outline means the story names the company. A dashed outline means it doesn't, but Claude expects a knock-on effect.
✓ ✗ – …
Each call is checked against how the stock has moved since the story, compared with the S&P 500 over the same days: right, wrong, too close to call, or too early to check. Select any company chip to see the price chart.

Treat that score as a sanity check, not proof. Many stories report a price move that has already begun, which flatters it, and a few days of prices is a small sample.

Data: news from Finnhub, headline tone from FinBERT, company links from Claude (Haiku 5.5), daily prices from Yahoo Finance. New stories, Claude's reads and the right-or-wrong checks update about every two hours, and an open page offers to load them when they arrive.
